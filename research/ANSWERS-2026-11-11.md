## Seasons to look at again

### 1. Shallots

**Changed. Medium confidence (was low).**

- A commercial source: Oldershaw, a Lincolnshire grower and one of a handful of UK shallot growers, supplies the supermarkets. It says the British season runs from September to the following June. The shallots are harvested, cured, then held in controlled-atmosphere stores (`oldershaw-shallots`).
- Now `in` weeks 36–43 (September–October, the cured new crop reaching market) and `store` weeks 44–52 and 1–26 (to the end of June).
- The new-crop start was "mid to late summer". That was harvest, not market, so it now follows Oldershaw. The store end moves from the end of March, which was extrapolated from the RHS, to the end of June.
- The in/store boundary isn't stated by the source. It follows the onion convention of about two months of new crop.

### 2. Braeburn

**Changed. Low confidence (unchanged).**

- Harvest is sourced: Adrian Scripps (Kent) harvests Braeburn from late September or early October to the end of October (`freshplaza-scripps-braeburn-a`, `-b`). The British apple harvest finishes in November with Braeburn (`caterer-apples-2016`).
- First sales: New Covent Garden expected the first Braeburns later in October 2025, and BAPL's varieties page starts the season in November.
- Now `in` weeks 43–48, the same convention as the other apples (`in` = harvest and first sales), then `store`.
- Storage to mid-August rests only on Defra's 2026 wholesale quotes for home-grown Braeburn. BAPL ends the season in May. Rule 2 lets availability evidence extend `store`, so it stays, at low confidence.
- The older "not fit to eat until December" sources (about 2005–08) and BAPL's season page (late December) are kept as discrepancies.

### 3. Milk-fed spring lamb and market morels

**Changed: both are now `in`, under rule 2.** No source names a season for either, so the availability evidence sets `in`.

- **Milk-fed lamb (weeks 14–17):** stays low. The only source is one producer, writing in 2014, and that producer's current pages don't list milk lamb. A search on 11 November 2026 found no other UK producer. The note says this line is a candidate for `no_supply` if no current producer turns up.
- **Market morels (weeks 18–22):** stays low, with one secondary source (a wholesaler).

### 4. Leeks, weeks 23–26

**The gap is real and now sourced. Weeks unchanged.**

- Farmers Weekly says British leeks are available nearly all year except June (`fwi-leeks-growing`).
- A Merseyside grower has a gap from mid-May until the new season at the end of June or start of July (`freshplaza-askew-leeks`).
- The British Leek Growers Association's chairman says harvest starts in June or July and can still run in May (`freshplaza-lga-casey`).
- In June 2026, a wholesaler reported British leeks finished and French and Spanish imports filling in until the UK new season in late June to early July (`freshview-crop-report-2026-06`).

### 6. Halibut

**North East leads.**

- It has no `in` weeks, and that's correct by rule 5. Atlantic halibut spawns from December to April (`fishbase-atlantic-halibut`), and Norway closes its fishery for spawning from 20 December to 20 April (`mcs-atlantic-halibut`).
