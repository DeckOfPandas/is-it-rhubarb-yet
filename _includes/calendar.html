{% comment %} The whole-year chart as one inline SVG. Expects: order, id. order "start": one row per variety, sorted by the first week it is in season, so the chart reads top to bottom as the year unfolds. order "group": rows gathered by culinary family, each family sorted by the food's first in-season week, a food's varieties together. order "alpha": by food name, then variety, a food's varieties together. The calendar page bakes the chart once, by start; assets/charts.js reorders its rows in place for the other two, by the same keys (orderRows), so the keys here and there must agree. A row is labelled with the food, and a named variety follows in muted brackets as tools/bake.js names it, "Rhubarb (forced)", "Apples (Cox)". A ring that does not give the verdict (`lead: false`: another coast than a fish's South West, a glasshouse, a wild ring beside a market one) has no row; the food's page shows it. A row with a short name (foods.yml's `short:`, on the variety for the whole row, or on the food for its name) carries it as a second label, which CSS shows in place of the first below 700px and nowhere else. The chart is drawn at its own size, never scaled, so its words are never smaller than they are set; where it is wider than the page it scrolls sideways. It is three SVGs sharing one set of coordinates, each viewBox cut to its part: the food names in a column that sticks to the left, the weeks beside them, and over both a header of month names that sticks to the top of the viewport, with a blank corner (a plain div) over the names. A row is two links to its food's page, its name and its weeks; only the name takes focus. JavaScript adds the "today" marker, keeps the header level with the rows when they are scrolled sideways, and opens the chart scrolled to today.
