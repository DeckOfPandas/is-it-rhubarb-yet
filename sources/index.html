--- title: Source data description: Where the seasons on this site come from, and how to get the data. permalink: /sources/ ---

Source data

UK seasons from the New Covent Garden Market calendar, checked against Defra's wholesale figures (which also give the glasshouse seasons); the fish and shellfish from three coasts, Flying Fish Seafoods in Cornwall, Brighton & Newhaven Fish Sales and a Northumberland chart, and nationally from Seafish; the shooting seasons from BASC; wild fungi from First Nature; foraged plants from the Forestheart poster; apples from British Apples & Pears; and lamb, goose and turkey from their own growers' bodies. A fish's verdict is its South West season; the other coasts are on its page. The herbs, a few wild and foraged foods, some shellfish and the meats sold all year are still common knowledge: each of those pages says so until it has been checked against a named source.

Dates say "about" because the data is weekly and the weather is not. A season starting in a week means the first week it is normally worth asking for, in England, broadly; Scotland runs later.

The data

All of it as JSON: one object per food with its name, slug, group, colour, its sources (or one older source) and a list of varieties, its rings: a variety, or a ring of the food itself (ring: a coast, a growing method, a legal zone or a part of the plant), each with its own sources and its weeks as [from, to, state] runs on a 52-week year where week 1 starts on 1 January and state is in, avail (just coming in or going out) or store (sold from store). A ring marked "lead": false is shown but does not give the verdict. It and the calendar feed are CC BY 4.0; say where it came from.

Calendar feed

Subscribe to the season starts in your calendar app and every food's season arrives in it as an all-day event on the week it starts, "In season: Rhubarb (forced)", every year. Add it by its address, rather than by downloading the file, and it stays up to date by itself.

Something wrong? Open an issue with the food, the week and where you looked.
