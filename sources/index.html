Source data

The dates I've used almost all come from people who specialise in food. Each individual food's page lists its own sources, and a full list is below.

Fruit and veg. New Covent Garden Market's British seasonal fruit and veg calendar. Defra's wholesale prices for home-grown produce give the glasshouse dates, because a price is only A Thing while a UK crop is actually on sale. A few corrections come from the RHS, British Apples & Pears, and growers.

Mushrooms and foraging. Cultivated mushrooms are from Covent Garden, and wild fungi from First Nature. Foraged plants are from the Forestheart foraging calendar and common knowledge*.

Herbs. General internet knowledge so far, supported by the much-loved herb bed in my garden. If you know a good source, tell me.

Fish and shellfish. Three fishmongers on three coasts: Flying Fish Seafoods in Cornwall, Brighton & Newhaven Fish Sales in Sussex, and Northumberland Seafood, in — you'll never guess — Northumberland. Also Seafish's national guide, and the law where there is one: for example, native oysters can't be sold from mid-May to early August, and wild salmon can't be sold at all. A fish's verdict answers for all its coasts together; each coast's own season is on the fish's page.

Fish sustainability ratings are those stated in the Marine Conservation Society's Good Fish Guide, calculated according to their published methods.

Meat and game. Shooting seasons are from BASC. Lamb, goose and turkey are from industry bodies and butchers who rear and sell them. Carbon figures and the meat sustainability labels worth looking for are still to come; where they will come from is listed below.

Dates are said in words rather than days because the data is weekly or monthly, whereas the weather does its own thing.

Cool, give me allll the numbers

Here is all my food data as JSON. It uses a 52-week year where week 1 starts on 1 January, and the last day or two of December become honorary week 52. My compilation of source dates is CC BY 4.0: use it however you like, just say where it came from. The sources have their own terms: Defra's prices and the legislation are public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0, and the quotations on the food pages remain their authors' and are not mine to license.

Calendar feeds

You can subscribe to food season starts (rounded to the week) in your calendar app! How very exciting! Apple, Google, .ics file.

Unless you, for your own reasons, want several hundred food season events per year to suddenly arrive in your calendar, add a feed for a particular food rather than the whole calendar. But you do you. Links are on individual food pages.

Add a feed by its address if you can, rather than by downloading the .ics file, so it magically stays up to date by itself.

The feeds and verdicts are rebuilt approximately daily, according to the whims of GitHub Actions. Google Calendar will check about once a day.

Spotted something wrong? I'd be grateful if you could open a GitHub issue.

* "Common" knowledge isn't the same as safe and legal knowledge. Don't eat anything you find growing unless:

  1. you are trained, and absolutely certain of what it is and the safety of where it's growing; and
  2. you are foraging strictly for personal use; and
  3. you are certain you know how not to damage the rest of the crop, its future abundance, and its ecosystem.

Under the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981, it is illegal to uproot any wild plant without the permission of the landowner or occupier. Picking leaves, flowers, fruit or fungi, rather than uprooting the plant, is generally allowed for your own use, except for protected species and on protected sites.
